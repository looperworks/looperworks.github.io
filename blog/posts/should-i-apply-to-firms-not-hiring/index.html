Should I Apply to Firms That Aren't Hiring?

Yes. And here is why most of the best opportunities never get posted.

Yes. You should apply. Most architecture positions at small and mid-size firms are never posted publicly. If you only apply to firms with job listings, you are missing most of the opportunities.

Here's why. Small firms hire based on project flow, not planning. A firm gets a project in March. They need someone now. They don't have time to post a job, wait two weeks for applications, and interview people. They call someone whose work they know. They reach out to someone who reached out to them. They hire someone fast.

The hidden job market in architecture

If your portfolio is already in a principal's inbox, you're first in line for that March project. If you're waiting for a job posting, you might never know about it at all.

Large firms post jobs because they have HR departments and formal hiring processes. Boutique firms almost never do. They hire through relationships, referrals, and cold outreach. This is especially true for entry-level and mid-level positions. If you want to work at a specific firm, cold email is more effective than waiting for a posting.

How to approach firms with no openings

Find firms whose work you actually respect. Use the Threshold firm search to build a list. Look at their websites. Do their projects excite you? Do they work at a scale or in a style you want to master?

Then reach out. Not with a formal job application. With a thoughtful cold email. Show that you've done research. Reference a specific project. Explain why their work matters to you. Ask for 15 minutes or to share your portfolio.

This is not difficult. Most design principals never get thoughtful emails from architecture students. When they do, they remember them. You stand out immediately.

Timing is everything

You probably won't hear back right away. That's okay. You might hear back in six months when they suddenly need someone and your email is the one they remember. You might hear back immediately because they have a project starting next month and they've been looking for the right person.

The worst case is they don't respond. You moved on. You sent cold emails to 15 firms. One of them responds. That's a success.

Use cold email as your advantage

Most people only apply to posted jobs. Most people are afraid of cold email. Most people don't reach out to firms they actually want to work for. This is your competitive advantage. Cold email works because so few people do it thoughtfully.

For a framework on how to write cold email that actually works, read the cold email guide. The principles are the same whether the firm has a posting or not.
