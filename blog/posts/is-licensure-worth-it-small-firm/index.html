Is It Worth Getting Licensed If I Want to Work at a Small Firm?

Licensure changes what you can sign, not what you can design. But it changes how you are valued.

Yes. But for a different reason than you might think. At a small firm, a licensed architect can stamp drawings. This means the firm does not need to hire a consultant or keep a separate licensed person on staff for that role. That makes you significantly more valuable.

What licensure actually means

Licensure does not change what you can design. You can design anything as an unlicensed architect. What changes is what you can sign. A licensed architect can put their stamp on construction documents. This legal authority means clients can rely on those documents for permitting and construction.

At a small firm without an in-house licensed architect, every project requires hiring a consultant to stamp drawings. That costs money. That creates scheduling delays. A licensed person on staff eliminates this. You become essential infrastructure, not just another designer.

The timeline and cost

Most people take 1-2 years to complete licensure after graduation. The process involves accumulating Architecture Experience Program (AXP) hours, passing the Architect Registration Examination (ARE), and meeting state-specific requirements. AXP requires 3,740 hours of documented experience, which most people accumulate while working full-time.

The total cost is manageable: roughly $500-800 for exams, plus study materials. Many firms help pay for this. Some firms offer tuition reimbursement or study time as part of their benefits package.

Ask about licensure support

When you're evaluating firms, ask whether they support licensure. Do they cover exam costs? Do they give time off for studying? Do they reimburse for exam prep courses? Some firms make this easy. Others expect you to do it on your own time.

Use the Threshold firm search to find firms that offer licensure or ARE support. This is listed under the hiring hints filter. It's a concrete sign that the firm is invested in your development and understands that licensure makes you more valuable to them.

The practical value

Licensed architects typically earn more than unlicensed ones. At a small firm, you're more promotable. You have more authority in client conversations. You can lead projects in ways that unlicensed architects cannot. The credential opens doors.

If you plan to stay in architecture long-term, licensure is worth getting. It changes your career options. It changes how you're compensated. It changes how much responsibility you can take on. At a small firm, it makes you indispensable.
